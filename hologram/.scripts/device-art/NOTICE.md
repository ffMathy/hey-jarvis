# Device art

Where the frames in the showcase clips come from, and what may be done with them. Everything here
was fetched and prepared by [`../prepare-device-art.sh`](../prepare-device-art.sh); nothing was
drawn by hand and nothing was traced from product photography.

The clips these are used in are `docs/jarvis-on-a-phone.*` and `docs/jarvis-on-a-watch.*`, rendered
by [`../render-showcase.ts`](../render-showcase.ts).

## `pixel-10-pro.png` — the phone

Google's own device art for the Pixel 10 Pro, the same frame Android Studio wraps a screenshot in.
Taken from the Android Open Source Project:

- **Source**: [`artwork/resources/device-art-resources/pixel_10_pro/back.webp`](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/tools/adt/idea/+/refs/heads/mirror-goog-studio-main/artwork/resources/device-art-resources/pixel_10_pro/)
  in `platform/tools/adt/idea`
- **Copyright**: The Android Open Source Project
- **Licence**: Apache License 2.0
- **Changes**: converted from WebP to PNG. Otherwise unmodified.

The frame has a screen-shaped hole through it, with the hole-punch camera drawn *inside* the hole,
so the renderer draws the screen first and this over the top. The hole's position comes from the
`layout` file that ships beside the art: 1280×2856 at (59, 60) with a corner radius of 99, in a
1410×2968 frame.

## `pixel-watch-3.svg` / `pixel-watch-3.png` — the watch

Google publishes device art for every Pixel phone but **none for the Pixel Watch**; Android Studio
frames a Pixel Watch screenshot with a generic round bezel that has no crown and no band. This is a
community vector instead.

- **Source**: [Google Pixel Watch 3-45 (Matte Black + Obsidian).svg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Google_Pixel_Watch_3-45_(Matte_Black_%2B_Obsidian).svg)
  on Wikimedia Commons
- **Author**: [Mliu92](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/User:Mliu92)
- **Licence**: [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/)
- **Changes**: one, marked by a comment at the top of the committed SVG — the screen circle's light
  grey fill is replaced with black. The PNG is that SVG rasterised with `cairosvg`.

The fill is blacked out because the renderer paints Jarvis over the screen rather than through it,
and any pixel its antialiasing does not quite reach showed up as a pale ring around the whole
screen. Black there, and there is nothing left to show.

**This licence reaches the clips.** CC BY-SA 4.0 is share-alike, so
`docs/jarvis-on-a-watch.gif` and `docs/jarvis-on-a-watch.webm` — which contain this artwork — are
themselves available under CC BY-SA 4.0, attributed to Mliu92 for the watch frame. That applies to
those two files, not to any source code in this repository.

Unlike the phone, the watch's screen is a filled circle drawn on top of the case rather than a hole
through it, so the renderer draws this frame *first* and paints the screen over it. The circle is
at (0.477754, 0.500986) of the frame with a radius of 0.381709 of its width, measured from the art
itself by the preparation script.

## `wallpaper.jpg` — what is behind the sheet

The only thing here that is not somebody else's: generated by `prepare-device-art.sh`, from a fixed
seed, so it comes out the same every time. No licence and nothing to attribute.

It is a handful of large soft shapes in one family of blues with a single warm accent, off-centre
and at angles that line up with nothing. It took four goes to get there, and the two dead ends are
worth recording because they are the obvious things to try:

- **A photograph** — Earth from Apollo 17, then Hubble's Westerlund 2. Both are wrong the same way.
  A photograph has detail and contrast in every square inch, and behind a hologram that is noise
  competing with the thing you are meant to be looking at. It is also the worst case for a GIF,
  where the whole clip shares one palette of at most 256 colours.
- **Flat concentric rings** — calm and cheap, and far too tidy to read as a wallpaper at all.

It fills the screen edge to edge rather than sitting in the middle of it, because a wallpaper with
black around it reads as a picture on a dark screen, and then there is no telling where the
background stops and the assistant begins.
